## Congratulations! Dr. Shen Ming-Hsueh Successfully Participates in NASA IMAP Mission Launch

The solar wind continuously blows outward, forming a giant protective bubble called the heliosphere. NASA's IMAP (Interstellar Mapping and Acceleration Probe) mission aims to deeply study the boundary region of the heliosphere and understand the interaction between solar wind and the interstellar medium.

### Mission Overview

The IMAP satellite successfully launched in 2025, carrying advanced scientific instruments and will conduct observations at the Lagrange L1 point—a location approximately 1 million miles from Earth where gravitational forces are in balance.

### The Center's Contribution

Dr. Shen Ming-Hsueh, a review committee member of our Center's CubeSat program, has been fully involved throughout this mission's scientific planning and instrument calibration work, adding another achievement to Taiwan's space science research on the international stage.

This successful launch marks a new milestone in international space exploration cooperation and demonstrates the important role of the Han-Min Hsu Center for Space Science and Technology in the global space science network.

### Scientific Significance

The IMAP mission represents a pivotal step in understanding the boundary between our solar system and the interstellar medium. The mission's findings will contribute significantly to our understanding of:

- Solar wind acceleration mechanisms
- The structure and dynamics of the heliosphere
- Cosmic ray propagation and modulation
- The interaction between solar and interstellar plasmas

This international collaboration exemplifies the strength of cooperative space science research. With the participation of talented Taiwanese scientists like Dr. Shen, Taiwan continues to play an active and important role in advancing global space science knowledge.
